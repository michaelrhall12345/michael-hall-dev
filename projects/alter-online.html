Online companion-creature game built in Unreal Engine 5.7 on the Gameplay Ability System. Players feed fruit to their companions, which reshapes both their stats and their bodies and drops essence byproducts back into the loop. As Freelance Gameplay Engineer at Studio Revive, I own the implementation of the core gameplay systems, working from the studio's design direction and alongside its 3D artist. Progression lives in GAS attributes and abilities, appearance lives in artist-authored skeletal morph targets, and my systems connect the two so new content can be added without touching code.

// systems.built
01 — GAS
Multi-Axis Morph System
Stat and appearance progression built on GAS. Creatures grow along three opposing morph axes plus a neutral growth path, and each axis maps attribute values onto artist-defined skeletal morph targets so stats and shape evolve together.
02 — Feature
Companion Creature Demo
Sole engineer on the companion-creature demo feature. Appearance changes are dynamic and combinatorial, driven entirely by GAS attributes and abilities, so no two creatures have to grow the same way.
03 — Loop
Feeding & Byproducts
The core loop: fruit fed to a companion applies its stat and morph changes through GAS, and the feeding produces essence byproducts that drop back into the world for the player to collect.
04 — GAS
Pickup & Equip
Item pickup and equip implemented as a GAS ability, routed through a dedicated interaction component. The first feature milestone on the project, and the base the feeding loop was built on.
05 — Arch
Identity vs. Behavior
Settled architecture rule for the project: the data asset defines what an item is, and the C++ class defines what it does. New fruit, items, and morphs are data; new mechanics are code.
06 — Pipeline
Artist-Driven Mapping
Morph targets stay in the artist's hands. The system binds what art authors to gameplay data, so new looks can be added without new engineering work.
